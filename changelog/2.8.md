# Changelog: 2.8

## [2.8.36] - 2026-11-12

### Changed
- With Pehkui installed, a portal that changes size (a scaling portal, or a portal on a ship Create: Pocket Sized has scaled) resizes you through Pehkui, so walking speed, jumping, step height, reach, falling and the rest follow your size, as they did in Immersive Portals before 1.21. Before, only your size and eye height changed: shrunk, you kept full speed and seemed to race about; grown, you crawled. The portal's share is a Pehkui scale of its own (`iportal:scaling`, `/scale reset iportal:scaling` clears it), multiplied with any other Pehkui scale such as `/scale set` or Pocket's harness, and reset on death as before. Sizes from a world played without Pehkui carry over when Pehkui is added, and a player's carries back when it is removed. Fall distance built up before the portal is carried at the new size, as Pehkui scales fall damage by size. Without Pehkui nothing changes.
- With Pehkui installed, the third-person camera and the reach for clicks through portals follow your Pehkui scale (from `/scale` or any mod), not just the vanilla scale attribute.
- The "reset your scaling made by portals" command stick clears the Pehkui scale when Pehkui is installed.

## [2.8.35] - 2026-11-12

### Fixed
- The part of a ship already through a portal is lit by the sky on its side: it took the sky light of the world the ship was leaving, so a ship coming out of the nether, a cave or a covered portal room was drawn dark past the portal until it was all the way through. Create's moving parts on that part (shafts, cogs, propellers) still take the old world's sky light, and only until the ship is through.

## [2.8.34] - 2026-11-11

### Added
- F3+U (vanilla's frustum capture, a debug key) now also freezes portals: while a frustum is captured, portals and what is drawn inside their views are culled from the captured camera, carried through each portal, instead of from the live one. Walking away after F3+U shows which portals, and which chunks and entities behind them, would be drawn from the captured spot; the frozen frustum's outline continues through the portals. Walking through a portal keeps the capture: your new surroundings are culled as the captured camera sees them through that portal, and the portal back shows the captured world as the captured camera sees it. Shift+F3+U releases it as before. Create's moving parts (Flywheel), ships, shadows and LODs are culled from the live camera, as in vanilla's capture.

### Fixed
- With Sodium, a portal's view draws only the chunks seen through the portal's opening again, instead of every chunk in the whole screen's view cone behind the portal out to the render distance. Sodium 0.6 and later test chunks against the view in a new way that Immersive Portals' cut to the portal opening was never hooked into (it was written for Sodium 0.5), so every portal on screen cost about as much as drawing the world a second time.

## [2.8.33] - 2026-11-10

### Fixed
- Without a shader pack, a portal fading at the edge of the portal draw distance no longer blanks or flickers for a frame the first time a portal fades after starting the game, and while the window is resized. Making room for the copy of what is behind the portal left the screen's own framebuffer bound instead of the world's, so the portal's view area was drawn to the screen.
- Multiplayer: ships seen through portals no longer vanish or leave a ghost when one player's view of a ship is topped up with chunks in the same tick another player leaves that ship's dimension. Ending the top-up switched off the dimension tag on Sable's packets for the rest of the tick, so a "stop tracking" meant for the leaving player could be applied in their new dimension. The tag is now a depth count, so a nested pair no longer ends the outer one.
- Singleplayer: portal rims (the frames that hold ships at frameless portals) no longer pile up in the physics library each time a world is left and joined again; a leftover rim could switch off a new rim's contacts with a ship. A world's rims are now removed with its physics scene.
- Servers without Sable report other mods' hooks that stopped matching (`[IPCompat]`), and no longer keep the classes it reads in memory all session.

### Removed
- Diagnostics from earlier test rounds, all settled: the `[IPSeatSync]`, `[IPDrive]` and `[IPUnloadSpin]` traces (at tag v2.8.32 if needed again), test-jar-only logs of client straddle sessions, the hand-off latch and hand-off meshing, `[IPDhGapFix]` logs, a Voxy renderer log line, and two stderr lines of the physics library.
- Code nothing reached: unused methods in the straddle, ship-portal, Sable and Create code, a redundant rope-packet redirect (`MixinRopePackets_RedirectedLevel`, covered by `MixinRopePackets_PlayerLevel`), an unregistered inherited Cardinal Components mixin and two mixin-plugin branches that matched nothing, and in the physics library a body-pair exclusion set that was never filled but was checked for every pair of ships each substep, the old 14-number clip region layout, an unused gravity field and a benchmark that did not compile. No change in play: physics is the same to the last digit.

## [2.8.32] - 2026-11-10

### Changed
- Physics backend: our physics library is now loaded in place of Sable's and driven by Sable's own Java classes, instead of a renamed copy of them loaded beside Sable's. Mods that hook Sable's physics classes now act on our physics too. Our four changes to the copy are mixins on Sable's classes (the fused step's world step, block collisions bound to their level, joints between ships, the link-error message), each doing nothing while Sable's own library runs; none is applied for a Sable version other than 2.0.6. Stock physics (kill switch `-Dip_aeronautics.stockPhysics=true`, an unsupported Sable, no library for the platform, another mod loading its own library there) is Sable's own library and code, unchanged. No change in play: physics is the same to the last digit.
- Create: Pocket Sized no longer switches ship straddling off. Its scaling runs on our physics; ships it scales cross portals in one step as before (our straddling reads a ship at 1x), every other ship straddles. A ship scaled while straddling leaves its straddle sessions. A Pocket Sized whose scale query has moved keeps stock physics.

## [2.8.31] - 2026-11-10

### Changed
- Physics backend: native code that came with IPSable's crate and that nothing called is gone (dormant bodies, parent frames, the joint-component query and rope portal seams in `natives/rapier`). No change in play: physics is the same to the last digit.

## [2.8.30] - 2026-11-10

### Fixed
- Ships passing through a portal into their own dimension keep their springs, propellers and other physics parts working. A ship loaded into a plot whose chunk had been looked up shortly before (often the plot a ship tied to it had just left, as two ships tied by a spring do when they cross together) had its block entities looked up in a stale chunk while Sable registered them, so Sable never ticked them: the spring was drawn but pulled nothing. Vanilla keeps the last four chunks looked up and clears them whenever its chunk map changes; Sable adds and removes plot chunks without clearing them. We now clear them whenever a plot gains or loses a chunk.

## [2.8.29] - 2026-11-10

### Added
- Colorwheel support (Flywheel under an Iris shader pack): Create's contraptions, trains and kinetic parts are drawn through portals, cut at the plane while crossing or straddling, and drawn on the far side, as with Flywheel without a pack. Checked with Colorwheel 1.3.2 and Complementary Unbound r5.9.3.

### Fixed
- With Colorwheel and a shader pack, trains, contraptions and other Flywheel parts behind the far portal no longer show through portal views (back-bleed). Colorwheel compiles Flywheel's programs from the pack's own sources, which never got IP's clip plane; its vertex programs now get it like Flywheel's own (`IPColorwheelPortalClip`). Shadows of parts behind the portal are kept, as with the pack's own shadow programs.
- With Colorwheel's indirect backend (its default), windmills, trains and other contraptions no longer vanish in portal views and around train crossings. Colorwheel places each contraption's bounds for culling with whatever matrix buffer was bound last instead of its own world's; with one world drawn per frame that is its own, but IP draws several, so contraptions were culled as if they stood where another world's contraptions are. Each world now binds its own before that pass (`IPColorwheelMatrixBinding`). A Colorwheel bug that IP's portal views bring out.
- With Colorwheel, a train crossing a portal is cut at the portal and its far half drawn on its own: the other dimension's windmills and contraptions no longer float around the near side while a carriage reaches through, and carriage copies not yet in place are hidden. Straddling ships' Create parts are cut at the portal too. IP's cuts went by Flywheel's own engine and embeddings, which Colorwheel replaces with its own; they now take either (`IPFlywheelEmbeddings`), and Colorwheel's programs get the per-embedding cut.
- With Joy of Creation, ropes of a ship in another dimension, seen through a portal, follow the ship again: dragged with the physics staff or moving, they were left frozen in the air from the moment the ship had crossed out of the player's world until the player followed or the ship moved. Joy of Creation's rope packet handler looks the rope's holder up in the player's own world, where the ship's old copy is still on the client at the same position; IP now handles those packets with the player in the packet's world (`IPRopePacketLevel`).
- Create shafts driven through a portal into another dimension turn in phase with the driving side again when Flywheel draws them (Flywheel on, or Colorwheel with a shader pack); they met the plane at a fixed angle off. Each dimension's level renderer counted its ticks from its own creation, and Flywheel animates by that count; every renderer now takes the player's count each frame (`ClientWorldLoader.alignRendererTicks`). Clouds and weather in portal views run on the same clock. Inherited from upstream IP.
- Create parts of a ship straddling a portal into its own dimension (a portal leading elsewhere in the same world) are drawn on the far side too: with Flywheel on (or Colorwheel) the part past the portal was missing, as the far half of Flywheel parts was drawn only for portals into other dimensions. With Colorwheel a far half is drawn once per pass, not again in its translucent and shadow phases.

## [2.8.28] - 2026-11-10

### Fixed
- No server crash ("this.wrapped" is null in `EntitySync.update`) when moving fast towards a Create contraption with a deployer on it. Pairing an entity with a player runs other mods' code, which can add or remove tracked entities: writing the contraption's spawn packet makes Create create the deployer's fake player, whose spawn search loads a chunk synchronously and so unloads the entities of chunks the player just left. IP's per-tick tracking passes went through the live map, whose iterator then ran off the table. Both passes (`EntitySync.update`, `EntitySync.tick`) go over a copy; an entity added during a pass is handled the next tick, one removed during it is skipped (not paired again as a ghost). Inherited from upstream IP. Player report, verified by the player.

## [2.8.27] - 2026-11-09

### Fixed
- With Dimensional Sable, ships crossing a portal into a dimension of another height (overworld to the nether or the End and back) no longer vanish into the ground and lose their block entities: chests, barrels and hoppers emptied, copycats blank, cannons and Create parts broken (reported by a player, Dimensional Sable 1.1.0). Sable stores a ship's sections by their index from the bottom of the world, so a move between dimensions re-keys them to keep the blocks at their height. Dimensional Sable makes the same correction on every plot load from the `min_section_y` it saves, and the two together put the ship's blocks 64 blocks below its block entities and its pose. The move now marks the plot as keyed for its destination, so Dimensional Sable adds nothing.

### Added
- A guard on every ship move through a portal: after loading the ship in its destination, every block it had is checked; if any are missing (another mod changing how Sable loads ships), the move is undone and the ship stays on its own side of the portal, where a straddle hand-off tries again a little later. A change in another mod then costs a crossing instead of a ship. At server start the log names every other mod's hook into Sable's ship loading, known ones at info and others as warnings.

## [2.8.26] - 2026-11-09

### Fixed
- A ship's Create contraptions (bearings, Offroad's borehead drill, pistons) start its crossing of a portal when they reach it, not when its hull does. A borehead hanging below its bearing reached the portal first, and until the bearing got there it was neither drawn past the portal nor cut at it: it vanished on the far side, and from the near side it showed over the view through the portal, with the terrain behind showing through it. Crossing detection read only the blocks in the ship's plot; a contraption's blocks are in its entity. It now also reads the contraption's blocks where they are at the moment, and the ship's bounds include the contraption's.
- With a shader pack (Flywheel off), Create contraptions and other entities placed on a ship straddling a portal (item frames, Contraption Diagrams) are cut at the portal, part drawn on each side, instead of drawn whole on the side their position is on (a bearing contraption's position is its bearing). They are placed against the portal by their bounding box, and one across it is drawn with its quads clipped at the plane, as block entities near the cut are. Without a shader pack, a contraption's part past the portal is drawn once (by Flywheel), not twice.

## [2.8.25] - 2026-11-09

### Fixed
- No crash ("Cannot invoke SectionRenderDispatcher.uploadAllPendingUploads() because the return value of LevelRenderer.getSectionRenderDispatcher() is null") when the client loads another dimension while a portal is being drawn (a world saved standing at a dimension stack floor portal crashed on every load; the End, which is loaded on the client on every login in that world for a reason not known yet, happened to load during the first portal views). ## [2.8.24] - 2026-11-09

### Fixed
- With LittleTiles and Sodium, the game no longer crashes as soon as a Sable ship comes into view ("Batching sections", NullPointerException in LittleTiles' `LittleRenderPipelineType.beforeCompileEnds`: "chunk" is null; reported by a player, LittleTiles 1.6.0-pre236, Sodium 0.8.13). LittleTiles finds the section of every compile in the level renderer's view area, and ships are compiled through vanilla's section compiler even with Sodium. Sodium builds that view area with a render distance of 0 and never moves it; IP replaced it with `ImmPtlViewArea`, which fills its sections only when the camera moves it, so every lookup returned null where vanilla's returns a section. With Sodium the view area is vanilla's again (nothing of IP's reads it there); without Sodium nothing changes. Verified in game.

## [2.8.23] - 2026-11-09

### Fixed
- Create contraptions on a ship straddling a portal (bearings, Offroad's borehead drill, rotating parts) are drawn on the far side of the portal with the rest of the ship and cut at the portal on the near side, with Flywheel on (no shader pack); before, they and the blocks on them (the borehead's vaults) showed only once the ship was all the way through (reported by a player: a borehead drill boring down through a dimension stack floor; verified in game). Create gives each contraption's Flywheel embedding the origin 0,0,0, outside the ship's plot, so the far half and the cut, which find a ship by the plot under an embedding's origin, never counted it as part of a ship. `IPFlywheelStraddleClip` now finds a contraption's ship by its entity's position, which Sable keeps in the plot.

## [2.8.22] - 2026-11-08

### Fixed
- Data generation (`runData`) in a mod's development environment no longer hangs for ever with this mod installed (report from a mod developer, 2026-11-07; reproduced with Create, Sable and Aeronautics, and verified the same way). Datagen runs as the client dist but has no `Minecraft` instance, and IP set up its client side in the mod constructor anyway: the peripheral mod's client init failed on the missing `Minecraft` (inherited from upstream), and the core's reached Veil's event platform and so `RenderStateShard`'s static init while Veil's and Registrate's constructors, on other mod-loading threads, were initializing its subclasses: a class-initialization deadlock. IP's client set-up is skipped in datagen (`IPModEntry.isRealClient`); a real client is unchanged.

## [2.8.21] - 2026-11-06

### Fixed
- With BadOptimizations' lightmap caching on (its default), lighting no longer freezes once a portal to another dimension is in use: the world stayed lit as by day after nightfall (full bright, as players reported) or dark after day came, until the player went through a portal, and worlds seen through a portal could be full bright (reported by players; reproduced and verified in game). IP keeps a lightmap per dimension; BadOptimizations cancels a lightmap's tick unless it is the first one ticked since the player's tick count changed (one shared "tick changed" flag), and that was always one of IP's other dimensions' lightmaps, ticked after `ClientLevel.tickEntities()` where the player's own is ticked before it. `DimensionRenderHelper` now sets vanilla's update flag on every lightmap it holds after each tick, and on a new one when it is created, so they all update every tick as in vanilla (that also saves a new portal view its first frame at full brightness without BadOptimizations).

- With BadOptimizations' sky colour caching on (its default), the sky and fog of a world seen through a portal follow its time, weather and the far portal's biome again (from the nether, the overworld's sky did not change after `/time set` until going back through; verified in game). BadOptimizations caches a sky colour per world and refreshes it only for the world that first asks for it each tick, the player's own; the one seen through a portal kept the colour it had when the player left it. Each portal pass now empties the viewed world's cached colour, so vanilla's is used, and puts it back after the pass (`IPBadOptimizationsCompat`).

## [2.8.20] - 2026-11-04

### Fixed
- Without Sodium, the world you are in is no longer drawn with the blocks of another dimension. With a ship in a world seen through a portal (the overworld seen from the nether, say), chunks of the player's world that were loaded or changed afterwards were built from the other world at the same coordinates: the overworld appeared in the nether and chunks vanished where blocks were placed or broken, until a respawn or a change of dimension (player report, 2026-11-04, 2.8.15). On the vanilla renderer Sable gives a ship the current world renderer's own section dispatcher, and `MixinSableChunkedRenderData_DispatcherLevel` pointed it at the ship's world for the ship's compile without pointing it back; it now restores the dispatcher's level when the compile returns (the compile tasks take their region snapshot at once, so the ship's sections still come from its own world). Present since 1.4.9.
- Opening a Ponder scene with a belt (the frogport's, for one) no longer crashes the game (player report, 2026-11-04, 2.8.15: `ClassCastException`, catnip's `DummyLevelEntityGetter` to `IELevelEntityGetterAdapter`). The scene ticks Create's belts in Ponder's own level, and the hook for belts through portals looked for portals there through `McHelper.traverseEntities`, which cast the level's entity getter to vanilla's. For a getter that is not vanilla's, it now asks the getter itself for the same chunk range (`LevelEntityGetter.get`); in Ponder that finds no portals, so the belt behaves as without IP. This covers every IP entity lookup in such levels, not only belts.

## [2.8.17] - 2026-11-04

### Fixed

- A player going through a portal within one dimension together with a ship they held (physics staff or handle) could end up a whole portal's distance past the other side, while the ship came out where it should (reported by players; reproduced with a custom portal). When a ship is handed off it takes the players right next to it along, and the server tells the client their new position. The client tests the player's eye path from one frame to the next against the portals, and that jump counted as a walk: when the other end lies roughly behind the first, the straight line from the first portal to the second passes through the first portal's opening, so the client went through it once more, and the server accepted that. A position the server sets in the player's own world now starts a new eye path, as a position in another world already did. Any other position the server sets near a portal (`/tp`, a dismount, a rejected crossing) is covered too.
- A player standing right in front of or behind a ship as it went through a portal, holding it or not, is no longer taken along at the moment the ship is all the way through. The ship's hand-off moved them by the ship's own displacement, which put a player who had not yet gone through just short of the far portal; through a portal with two faces (framed portals, nether portals) its back face then took them straight back, and they stood past the first portal without having gone through. Standing players now go through on their own, as everyone else does; seated riders are still carried with the ship.
- A ship that went through a portal into the same world was drawn somewhere else for a second, about a portal's distance from where it came out, before it showed in its place. While a ship straddles a portal the client cuts it at the plane and draws the part past it at the other end; once it is through, that goes on for a second, for the old copy, which is drawn a little behind the server. Within one world the ship arrives with the same identity in the same world, so the new copy was cut and drawn through the portal once more for that second. The cut now ends as soon as the new copy is there, also when the new copy arrives before the message that the ship is through.
- A ship going fully through a portal into the same world vanished for a few frames: the old copy was removed at once and the new one came 40 to 60 ms later. As for a portal into another world, the old copy is now kept until the new one is there, and the new one's blocks are built for drawing at once instead of in the background, which still left it missing for a frame or more as it came out towards the player.
- Riding a ship through a portal into the same world while dragging it with the physics staff, the player came out where they should but the ship ended up a portal's distance further on. Sable names a ship by its plot in the position a player standing on it sends, and the ship kept its plot when it moved within one world: a position the player's client sent from the ship before it went through was read against the ship already through, which put the player through the portal on the server before their own crossing arrived. That crossing was then refused as too far, so the staff never learned of it and pulled the ship a portal's distance away. A ship moving within one world now goes to another plot, as it may when moving to another world, so nothing meant for the ship that left reaches the one that arrived; the physics staff and handles find the moved ship by its new plot.

## [2.8.15] - 2026-11-03

### Fixed

- Blocks placed or broken around a Create Teleporters portal base that had just been broken snapped back at once, and came back as placed only after rejoining (reported by a player; `/forceload remove` did the same). IP keeps a player's chunks loaded with its own chunk tickets, which this mod had made vanilla's `FORCED` type at the same level and key as a forced chunk's, and the game keeps one copy of equal tickets: un-forcing the chunks around the base also removed IP's tickets there. The chunk in the middle stopped ticking, and the game sends block changes only from ticking chunks, so the player's own changes never came back to the client; mobs there stood still too. It lasted until the player went far away. IP's tickets have their own type again (`imm_ptl`, as upstream), with the same loading and ticking as before, and IP no longer removes other mods' or `/forceload`'s forced chunks when the player leaves them.

## [2.8.14] - 2026-11-03

### Fixed

- Entities standing on a ship that flies up through a dimension stack's ceiling (into the Aether, say) fell through the ship as soon as it reached the portal, and a player walking or jumping up into it fell through a floor still below the plane. An entity goes through when its eyes cross, and through a portal in a ceiling its feet are then still on the other side, on the deck there; IP's collision lets it stand on that world's blocks behind the portal, but not on its ships. The ships behind each portal an entity touches now collide too, mapped through the portal, for their part behind its plane and in its opening (`IPStraddleCut.behindPortals`), and a player standing on one is not kicked for floating.
- A ship dragged with the physics staff could be thrown a whole portal's distance away when its crossing carried the player along with it: under a dimension stack, 384 blocks down into the void, where it was lost. The drag (and a held handle) follows the player through a portal only on the player's own teleport, and a player carried through by the ship's hand-off never had one, so the staff kept pulling towards the old world's point. Players carried with a ship now count as going through its portal, on the server and their client (`IPShipRiderCarry.takeCarriedPlayers`, `IPPhysicsStaffHooks.onShipMoved`).
- Mobs seated on a ship lost their seat whenever the ship crossed a portal: slowly they dropped through the deck, fast they were thrown off. The crossing deletes the old ship, which kills its seat entities; players were sat down again on their seat afterwards, mobs were put above the middle of the ship with its speed and stayed seated only if they happened to land on a seat. Mobs are now put at their seat on the arrived ship and sat down on it the next tick, and the crossing takes every rider of a seat on the ship wherever it is (on a fast ship Sable puts a rider at its seat a tick behind, outside the space the crossing collects entities from) (`IPShipRiderCarry`).
- A mob that crossed a dimension stack's ceiling or floor within one move (more than its height in a tick, as when thrown up by a fast ship) never touched the plane and stayed on its old side: IP sent mobs through a global portal only while touching it. Their eyes' move is now tested against the world's global portals each tick.

## [2.8.12] - 2026-11-03

### Fixed

- Neo Voxy (NHblock-Johnsnow's NeoForge port of Voxy, 0.5.0-alpha.12): LODs through portals. Its `setupViewport` takes other arguments than Roxy's Voxy, so the hook that gives each portal view a Voxy viewport of its own never matched; Voxy was then skipped in portal passes, and with a shader pack the pack composited that dimension's last LODs, a frozen ghost image in the portal. The hook now matches Neo Voxy's too, and the hook that keeps a portal pass from moving Voxy's distance tracker also covers `renderOpaqueNow`, where Neo Voxy moves it. A render system's first draw of a frame is now judged at the draw, not at the viewport setup (Neo Voxy with a pack sets a viewport up twice in a pass), and nothing of this runs in Iris's shadow pass, where Voxy draws nothing.
- Neo Voxy with Create tracks through a portal within one dimension: the game hung once the two portal tracks were linked, when the far portal was on a ship. Neo Voxy draws distant tracks itself and took the link between the two portals for a straight track from one to the other; to the far portal's place in the ship's storage, millions of blocks away, that track was too long to ever finish. Portal links are now left out of Neo Voxy's distant tracks (which also removes a stray track drawn between nearby linked portals in the distance).
- Neo Voxy's distant tracks, Create machines, beacon beams and lasers in views into another dimension: they belong to the player's world only, but were drawn in every view, so a view from the Overworld into the Nether showed the Overworld's distant rails and machines at their Overworld coordinates (and hid the Nether's own rails), while distant beacon beams and lasers in the player's world vanished whenever such a portal was in view. They now keep to the player's own view. Machines in chunks of a world seen through a portal are no longer frozen into the player's world as the chunks unload.
- Neo Voxy shaded the faces of another dimension's LODs as the player's dimension (a Nether made from the Overworld got the Overworld's darker undersides); each dimension now gets its own.
- Neo Voxy on a server: chunks under ships were all sent again to every player tracking the ship, and chunks the player had not loaded were sent and never unloaded on the client, slowly using up memory as ships flew about. Only their light is sent now, for the chunks the player does not have. Neo Voxy's distant tracks also skip a portal link to a ship that reaches the client before our list of portal links does.

## [2.8.11] - 2026-11-03

### Changed

- "Dimension Stack View Distance" is now **Portal Draw Distance** (`portalDrawDistance`, 0-64 chunks) and applies to every portal: how far away a portal still shows what is beyond it. Every portal now fades out over the last quarter of that distance instead of vanishing at once, as a dimension stack's floor and ceiling already did. At 0 (the default) it is the render distance, or 100 blocks with a shader pack, as before. The fork's fixed 100-block cut-off under a shader pack (`MixinPortalRenderer_ShaderDistanceCull`) is gone. Regular portals stop at the render distance; a dimension stack's floor and ceiling are drawn past it with Distant Horizons or Voxy, now with a shader pack too (at most 4 times the render distance, where the view's far plane lies). A value set for the old option carries over. "Portal View Distance" is now **View Distance Through Portals**, same option (`portalViewDistance`).

### Fixed

- A portal more than 8 chunks from the player showed only sky once its far side unloaded: the server loads the far side of every portal within the player's view distance now (while its performance is good), as far out as the client draws portals.
- With View Distance Through Portals set and Distant Horizons, the fog at the end of a portal's view vanished as the player stepped back from the portal: once that reach passed the render distance the view lost its cut, and DH turns vanilla's fog off where it draws LODs, so the far side's terrain showed without fog. The terrain fog and the LODs of such a view now end in fog at that reach (Iris's `dhRenderDistance` with a shader pack, DH's own fog without one, `IPDhPortalFog`).

## [2.8.10] - 2026-11-02

## [2.8.0] - 2026-10-31
