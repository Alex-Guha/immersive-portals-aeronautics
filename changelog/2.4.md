# Changelog: 2.4

## [2.4.25] - 2026-10-20

### Fixed
- At a ship-mounted portal, a pipe with no pipe on the other side looked cut off at the portal plane (the rest of the pipe, drawn past the plane at world portals, was missing there). It is now drawn both ways round: for a pipe on the ship, in the fixed side's portal block; for a pipe at the fixed side, in the ship's portal block, moving with the ship.

## [2.4.24] - 2026-10-20

### Fixed
- With a shaft on both sides of a portal, the two half shafts met at the portal plane turned to different angles, so the shaft looked twisted where it crosses (with no shaft on the far side it was fine). Create turns every other shaft 22.5 degrees, by its position, so the two sides' shafts started out at different angles. The side driven through the portal is now drawn shifted to match the other side, its whole network alike, so cogs on it still mesh.

## [2.4.23] - 2026-10-20

### Added
- Shafts with a motor (or any generator) on each side of a portal, turning the same way: the two sides now act as one network, as they would with no portal between them. Both networks show the summed capacity and summed stress, and both overstress together. If one side turns faster it drives the other and overpowers its generators, as Create does when two networks are joined. Turning against each other breaks the slower side's end block, as Create breaks the block that joins two such networks. Every pair now shares stress this way: before, the driven side got only what the driving side had to spare, and its own generators did not help the driving side.
- Fluid pipes through ship-mounted portals: a pipe on a ship next to the ship's own portal joins the pipe at the same spot on the fixed far side, and back, as shafts do (pumping either way, pressure carried on past the portal).

### Changed
- Shafts on ships only pair through a portal mounted on the same ship (with a shaft at the fixed far side, or the other way round). 2.4.22 also let a ship's shaft held against a world portal, or a world shaft against a ship-mounted portal, pair within a tolerance; those no longer pair.

### Fixed
- No half shafts were drawn at ship-mounted portals, on either side (the whole portal pair was skipped). The fixed side now gets them in the world; on the ship they are drawn with the ship's own visuals, so they move with it, and the half past the plane goes to the other side when no shaft is there.

## [2.4.22] - 2026-10-20

### Added
- Shafts through portals work with any kinetic block at either end: gearboxes, gearshifts, clutches, sequenced gearshifts, generators (a hand crank or a water wheel that is not turning is driven like a shaft), machines, and other mods' blocks. The far end is now turned the way Create turns any block from a neighbouring shaft: its Create source is the portal block in front of it, so blocks that care which side their power comes from (a gearbox's directions, a gearshift's inverted side, a clutch) treat the portal side as the input. Stress is shared at the network level, so it no longer depends on the block either.
- Shafts on Sable ships through portals: a shaft on a ship next to a portal mounted on the same ship drives (or is driven by) the shaft at the same spot on the far side, and keeps doing so while the ship flies, falls or tilts, since the two move together. A ship can also be held with a shaft against a world portal, and a world shaft can meet a ship-mounted portal; these join when the shafts line up within about 10 degrees and hold up to about 20 degrees and half a block, and let go (stopping the far side) when the ship drifts away. Logged as `[IPPortalShafts] ... no longer drives ...: the shafts no longer line up`.

### Changed
- A driven end is no longer turned into a Create generator. If its own side has a faster motor, that motor takes the end over and the portal lets go (logged `its own side took it over`), instead of the two acting as joined generators. Driven ends saved by 2.4.19-2.4.21 stop once when loaded and are driven again within a second.

### Fixed
- Turning the portal's drive back on (a motor put back) left both networks showing no stress from machines on the far side, so the far side could overdraw the near side. Create now recounts the network's stress whenever the portal's share changes.

## [2.4.21] - 2026-10-20

### Fixed
- The half shafts in portal blocks (2.4.19) z-fought with the portal where they meet its plane, seen from the side of the portal away from the shaft. Their end at the block centre lies exactly in the portal plane; that face is now left out of the model (as for the portal tracks' rail ends), so the shaft simply runs into the portal.

## [2.4.20] - 2026-10-20

### Changed
- Declared incompatible with Create Transferables (mod id `transferables`): it carries Create shafts and fluid pipes through portals, which this mod does itself, and the two act on the same blocks. NeoForge now refuses to launch with both installed and names Create Transferables as the one to remove.

## [2.4.19] - 2026-10-20

### Added
- Create shafts through portals. A shaft (or any kinetic block with a shaft on that face: cogwheels, encased shafts, gearboxes, motors, machines) running into a portal turns the shaft at the same spot on the other side, the same way round as one continuous shaft would, at the same speed. Either side can hold the motor. The two sides share stress like one network: machines on the far side count against the near side's capacity, and both overstress together. Stopping the motor or breaking a shaft stops the other side; a side that is unloaded keeps turning, as in Create when a generator's chunk is unloaded, and both sides keep their state over a reload. A loop through portals (shafts that lead back to their own side) never keeps itself turning without a motor. Works with nether-style and frameless portals (not ship-mounted, scaled or tilted ones). The driven end becomes a Create generator, so if its side also has its own motor, the two behave as two Create generators joined by a shaft (the faster wins, opposite directions break a block). The far end must be a block that can be driven: shafts, cogwheels, encased shafts and most machines, not a gearbox, gearshift, clutch or generator. Logged as `[IPPortalShafts] ... drives ... through a portal` and `... no longer drives ...`; `-Dip_aeronautics.debug.portalShafts=true` logs pairs and shared stress. Headless `shafts-through-portal`, `shafts-portal-loop`, `shafts-portal-loop-late`, `shafts-unload` tests.
- In a nether-style portal, the half block between a shaft and the portal plane gets a turning half shaft, and so does the far portal block when there is no shaft on the far side, so a shaft looks continuous through the portal instead of cut a block short.

## [2.4.18] - 2026-10-20

### Fixed
- Placing or breaking a pipe at a portal with no pipe on the other side: the far half of the pipe (seen through the portal) now appears and disappears in the same frame as the near half, instead of up to half a second later. It was only looked for every 10 ticks, and its chunk section was then re-meshed in the background; it is now looked for before the next frame whenever a pipe or portal block changes, and re-meshed at once when the player made the change, as the near half is.

## [2.4.17] - 2026-10-20

### Fixed
- A Create train carrying a ship (any Sable object resting on a carriage) could vanish on its next trip through a portal, dropping the ship. When the ship was handed off to the other dimension, it took everything within a block of its hull along as cargo, including the carriage under it: the carriage's copy in the old dimension was moved into the new one, where Create's own copy (same UUID) already stood, so it was refused and lost. Create then saved the lost copy as an empty entity, and the next time it needed a copy of that carriage in a dimension (the way back) it found nothing to build one from and deleted the whole train, blocks included. A ship hand-off now leaves train carriages and their riders to Create, which moves them through the portal itself (logged as `[IPShipPortal] ship ... leaves N train carriage copy(ies) and rider(s) under it to Create`).

## [2.4.16] - 2026-10-20

### Fixed
- Ships joined by a swivel bearing (or any other joint between ships) could stay half through a portal for only 5 seconds: a ship wholly through waited at most 100 ticks for its partner, then crossed alone and came apart from it. That limit is for roped ships, which can drift a rope's length past the portal; a joint keeps its ships close to the portal, so jointed ships now wait for as long as the joint holds, and a bearing pair rests in a portal like any one ship straddling it. Groups with ropes keep the 5 seconds. Headless `handoff-swivel-hold` test.

## [2.4.15] - 2026-10-19

### Fixed
- A pipe going into a portal with no pipe on the other side looked cut off at the portal plane: the near side fills the half block up to the plane, and past it the portal shows the far world, which had nothing there. The far portal block now gets the rest of the pipe: Create's connection piece from the plane to the block face and an open end's rim there (`IPCreatePortalPipeStubs`). The portal blocks themselves are never drawn, so the piece is added to the far world's chunk mesh (NeoForge's `AddSectionGeometryEvent`, which Sodium runs too); which portal blocks get one is found on the client every 10 ticks, and it disappears once a pipe is placed there.

## [2.4.14] - 2026-10-19

### Added
- Create's fluid pipes work through portals (bugs.md #5). A pipe end that opens onto a portal is joined to the pipe end at the same spot on the other side, as if the two were neighbours: fluid pumped into the portal comes out of the far pipe, and one pump on either side is enough (its pressure carries on past the portal with the pump range left over; pumps on both sides add up). Nothing is held in the portal: Create moves fluid straight from a network's source to its targets, and filling the portal end fills the far network's targets. Copper pipes placed against a portal block connect into it; at a nether-style portal (portal blocks), the pipe end has no rim and a pipe piece fills the half block up to the portal plane on each side, so the pipe runs through unbroken. Frameless portals pass fluid but get no filler piece; ship-mounted portals do not pass fluid yet. Headless `pipes-through-portal.txt`: a pump in the overworld pushing and one in the End pulling both fill a tank on the other side at the same 8 mB/tick as a 12-pipe line without a portal; removing the motors clears the pressure on both sides.

## [2.4.13] - 2026-10-19

### Changed
- Reverted 2.4.11 and 2.4.12's arrow changes: arrows still skipped past ship-mounted portals at some spots and angles. Arrows behave as in 2.4.10. The investigation is tabled; findings and suspicions are in `docs/plans/arrows-through-portals.md`.

## [2.4.12] - 2026-10-19

### Fixed
- Arrows shot through a portal on a ship often stuck in the ship behind the portal instead of crossing (bugs.md #2). IP looks for entities to teleport only 2 blocks around a portal, so a bow arrow (3 blocks a tick) that started a tick further out crossed the plane unseen; IP found it a tick late, past the plane, and its next move hit whatever was behind the portal. On a ship, Sable moves an arrow stuck in the ship into the plot, and IP then refused the teleport ("Entity is too far to teleport"). An arrow whose move in a tick crossed a portal now asks for its teleport itself (`MixinAbstractArrow`); IP still teleports it only once. Headless `arrow-ship-portal-wall.txt` (a wall half a block behind a ship portal): 14 of 16 arrows stuck in the ship in 2.4.10, 1 in 2.4.11, none now.

## [2.4.11] - 2026-10-18

### Fixed
- Arrows shot at a portal at an angle, or into a floor portal, sometimes stuck in the frame or came out of the other side as a dropped arrow (bugs.md #2). An arrow moves its whole tick (up to 3 blocks) in its own world, and IP teleports it only after the server tick, so a block just past the portal's plane (the side of the frame for an angled shot, the ground under a floor portal) caught it first; IP then teleported the stuck arrow and it fell out limp. When an arrow's move crosses a portal that teleports it, its block and entity hits now end at the plane (`MixinAbstractArrow`). Covers every arrow, spectral arrows and tridents; snowballs, eggs, potions and fireballs are not changed. Headless `arrow-portal-frame.txt`: 18 of 120 shots at a framed portal (near the edge at 45 to 70 degrees) arrived limp in 2.4.10, none now.

## [2.4.10] - 2026-10-18

### Changed
- Reverted 2.4.9's unbinding of broken ship portals (option `unbindShipPortalsOnBreak` and its debug hook removed). The cause was IP's frame search: it looked 128 blocks around the mapped position in the nether too, where vanilla looks 16, so a portal lit up to about a thousand overworld blocks from an empty nether frame linked to it. A ship portal broken and relit after flying far linked back to its old exit; ground portals did the same. The search now divides `portalSearchingRange` (still 128) by the destination's coordinate scale: 16 blocks into the nether, 128 out of it, as in vanilla. IP searches whole chunks, so into the nether it reaches 2 to 3 chunks around the mapped position. Existing config files keep working: the value is unchanged.

## [2.4.9] - 2026-10-18

### Added
- A portal on a ship that breaks forgets its other side (feature-requests.md #5). Lit again, it searched the other dimension near where the ship is now and found its old exit, still an empty frame within IP's 128-block search, so a ship carried a long way relinked to where it had been. The frame a broken ship portal leaves is now skipped by portals lit on ships: the new portal links to another frame nearby or builds one (`IPShipPortalUnboundFrames`, saved per dimension). The frame stays an ordinary frame for everything else, and lighting a portal in it from either side binds it again. Server option "Broken ship portals unbind" (`unbindShipPortalsOnBreak`, on by default); off is the old behaviour. Test hook `/ip_aeronautics shipportals debug setblock <pos> <block>`.

## [2.4.8] - 2026-10-18

### Changed
- A dimension stack's floor or ceiling fades out towards the edge of its view range instead of vanishing at once, and that range is the render distance again: 2.4.7 drew it from any distance with Distant Horizons, which cost a second world pass whenever the sky was in view. Over the last quarter of the range (at least 16 blocks) what is behind the portal is blended back over it, so the other dimension's blocks, LODs and clouds fade together (`IPGlobalPortalFade`, shader `portal_fade`). New client option "Dimension Stack View Distance" (`dimensionStackViewDistance`, chunks, 0 = render distance) to see further with DH. Not with an Iris shader pack or in views through other portals (hard cut there, as before).

## [2.4.7] - 2026-10-18

### Fixed
- A player riding a vehicle through a portal was lifted by the vehicle's seat height on every crossing: IP put the vehicle where its seat is instead of the seat height below the rider (`McHelper.getVehicleOffsetFromPassenger` left out the vehicle's riding position). With the Aether's parachutes (seat 1.6 blocks up) on a dimension stack this was a loop of gliding out through the Aether's floor and being lifted back in; boats and minecarts bumped less.
- Distant Horizons through a dimension stack's floor: from the Aether the overworld under the player was a hole until rising far enough. Sodium does not draw sections more than the render distance above or below the camera, which from the Aether is the overworld's whole surface, but the LOD cut in portal views (2.3.20) only looked at horizontal distance and cut the LODs there too. Chunks whose visible terrain is out of Sodium's vertical reach now keep their LODs (height band per chunk, `IPDhPortalDrawnChunks`).
- Distant Horizons through a dimension stack's ceiling: more than the render distance below an Aether stacked over the overworld, the whole Aether (LODs and blocks) vanished, as IP skips portals past the render distance. With DH, a dimension stack's floors and ceilings are now rendered from any distance (`IPDhPortalView.rendersBeyondVanillaRange`).

## [2.4.6] - 2026-10-18

### Fixed
- Dimension stack with the Aether: after 2.4.5 a player going up through the overworld's ceiling was frozen between the two worlds, unable to go up or down. The Aether's teleport was cancelled, but only after IP's own hook at the head of `ServerPlayer.changeDimension` had already treated it as a dimension change and taken the player out of every chunk and entity tracker, on every tick the Aether tried (and scheduled a portal conversion that logged "The player is not in the correct world"). The teleport is now refused in that hook, before any of it.

## [2.4.5] - 2026-10-18

### Fixed
- Dimension stack with Distant Horizons: the nether's roof showed over the distant overworld LODs, and the overworld's bottom over the nether's. DH draws its LODs into the colour buffer only, so beyond the vanilla terrain the stack's floor and ceiling (portals as wide as the world) passed the depth test and drew the other dimension over the LODs in front of them. DH's depth is now copied in every pass before it applies its LODs, and a portal's view area is cut where that pass's LODs are nearer than the portal (`IPDhLodOcclusion`, view-area shader `portal_area_lod`). A floor or ceiling hidden everywhere is no longer rendered at all, which saves rendering the other dimension every frame. Not with an Iris shader pack.
- Dimension stack with the Aether above the overworld: going up trapped the player in a "Descending from the Aether" loop. IP moves a player through the stack's ceiling when their eyes cross it, so they arrive with their feet under the Aether's floor, and the Aether sends any player whose feet are below its floor back to the top of the overworld, still moving up. Another mod's dimension change is now cancelled while the entity is partly through a dimension stack floor or ceiling that can teleport it (`VerticalConnectorTravelGuard`); falling out of the Aether's bottom is then the stack's seamless crossing. Log line: "[IPVerticalConnector] ... cancelled another mod's teleport".

## [2.4.4] - 2026-10-18

### Fixed
- A Create Aeronautics laser pointer or optical sensor near a portal showed through that portal's surface from the far side: its lens and beam were drawn in the view through the portal although they are on the near side of the far portal. They use Simulated's own shaders, which IP's portal clip does not reach; in portal views their whole drawing is now cut at the portal on the CPU (`IPLaserPortalsClient.renderClippedInPortalPass`).

## [2.4.3] - 2026-10-18

### Added
- Create Aeronautics lasers go through portals. A laser pointer's beam or an optical sensor's ray that reaches a portal goes on in the world on the other side, through up to four portals. A laser sensor over there lights up, and an optical sensor sees (and filters, and measures the distance to) the block behind the portal. Before, the beam went straight on through the world behind the portal. The beam is drawn up to the portal and goes on in the view through it and on the other side (`IPLaserPortals`). Scaled portals are left out (the beam goes straight through as before), and so is a far side your client has not loaded.

## [2.4.2] - 2026-10-18

### Fixed
- Ships joined by a Create Aeronautics swivel bearing (or a docking connector) came apart while going through a portal and snapped back together once both were through: the first one through was moved to the other dimension on its own, and the bearing lost its joint until its partner arrived. Jointed ships now wait for each other at the portal and cross in the same tick, as roped ships already did, and the bearing rebuilds its joint on the other side. Any joint between two ships counts (`IPShipJoints`, recorded by our physics backend). If the partner is not through within 5 seconds, the first ship crosses alone as before. The one-step crossing (straddling off) still moves them one at a time.

## [2.4.1] - 2026-10-18

### Fixed
- With Cirrus, looking through a portal into a world with a sun (from the nether into the overworld, say) made other portals' faces, translucent blocks and particles show through terrain, around the sun or across the whole screen. Cirrus clears depth after hiding the sun behind its clouds, and a clear ignores IP's portal stencil, so it wiped the depth of the world around the portal. Depth clears while a sky is drawn in a portal view now stay inside the portal (`IPPortalPassDepthClear`); the sun is still hidden behind Cirrus's clouds in portal views.

## [2.4.0] - 2026-10-17

First public release since 2.3.1. Groups the test builds 2.3.2-2.3.21 (details in their entries below): Create trains through portals (no carriage front flashing ahead, the driver keeps the controls, portal tracks glow with the frame's colour), Create Dynamic Lights ship lamps lighting the world, also across a portal; Distant Horizons LODs no longer showing through fences, stairs and slabs, in your own view or through a portal; blocks placed on ships showing without Sodium; starting without Sable or Create; and fixes for Petrolpark's Library, Netherstorm, Halcyon and mods that need Immersive Portals 6.0.7 or later.
